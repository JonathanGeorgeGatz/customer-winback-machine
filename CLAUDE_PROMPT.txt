"Build It With AI" · Episode 8 · The Customer Win-Back Machine
THE THREE PROMPTS, WORD FOR WORD, IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SENT.

Prompt 2 exists because the tool was wrong. Prompt 3 exists because I was.
Both mistakes are in the video, unedited.

==============================================================================
PROMPT 1 — THE SPEC (with my 90-day mistake already in it)
==============================================================================
Build me a single-file web tool that finds the customers who stopped coming
back, so I can try to win them back before they're gone for good.

GOAL
I run a small service business. My point-of-sale can export every sale as a
CSV, and that export is the only honest record I have of who still comes in.
I want to paste that export into a page and be told: who has quietly stopped
showing up, what their business was worth to me in a year, and what I should
send them. Right now nobody notices a regular has gone missing until we
wonder out loud about them, which is usually months too late.

DATA — one pasted CSV, straight from the till
  Date (YYYY-MM-DD) | Customer | Email | Service | Amount
A customer is identified by their email address. Names are for display.

RULES
- A customer who hasn't been in for 90 days has lapsed. Flag them.
- Rank lapsed customers by what their business is worth in a year, biggest
  first, because I can't chase everyone and I shouldn't try.
- For each one show: name, email, last visit, how long they've been quiet,
  and what they're worth.
- Totals at the top: how many customers I have, how many have lapsed, and
  what the lapsed ones are worth put together.
- Then the part that actually saves me time: pick a customer and generate
  the win-back message — warm, short, specific to them, with their name and
  their usual service in it, and give me three versions: one with a come-back
  offer, one that's just a check-in with no discount, and one that asks what
  went wrong. I will paste it into my own email. Do not send anything.

EVERYTHING STAYS ON MY MACHINE
This is my customer list. The file must work offline, make no network
requests of any kind, and have no analytics, no fonts fetched from anywhere,
no API keys. One HTML file I can open by double-clicking it.

PROMPT 2 — THE FIXES THE CHECKER FORCED: IDENTITY, SWALLOWED ROWS, THE MONEY

==============================================================================
The independent checker and the tool disagree about how many customers I
have, who has lapsed, and what every single one of them is worth. Three
problems, and the tool owns two of them.

ONE — identity. The tool treats the email as a raw string, so
"mika.sorensen@example.com", "Mika.Sorensen@example.com" and the same
address with a space in front of it are three different people. That is one
woman. She has been coming in for two years, she was here nine days ago, and
two of her three fragments are sitting on the lost-customer list. My till has been
through two tablets and a POS migration; capitalisation is not identity.

FIX: a customer is the email address lowercased with the whitespace trimmed.
Display whatever name appeared most recently. Two rows on the same day for
the same customer are one visit — a groom plus a nail trim is not two trips.

TWO — the rows the tool swallowed. Three walk-ins have no email at all, one
row has a date of 2026-13-05, and one is a negative refund line. The tool
skipped all five without a word. Never do that. List every row you could not
use, with the row number and the reason, so I can see what the report is not
built on.

THREE — the money, and this one was my fault for not defining it. The tool
ranks by lifetime spend, so a customer who spent two thousand dollars over
two slow years outranks one who spends twelve hundred in one year. "Worth
to me in a year" means a YEARLY RATE:

    yearly value = total spend x 365 / their tenure in days,
    tenure = first visit to last visit, plus one of their usual gaps,
    never less than 365 days.

So a two-year customer who spent $2,040 is about a thousand a year, and a
brand-new customer's first $85 does not get multiplied into a fantasy. Round
money half away from zero. Say "a year of them" on screen, not "value",
so nobody mistakes it for a lifetime number.

PROMPT 3 — THE ONE THAT MATTERS: EVERY CUSTOMER AGAINST THEIR OWN RHYTHM

==============================================================================
The 90-day rule was my idea and it is wrong in both directions, so take it
out and replace it with each customer's own rhythm.

Evelyn Hartree is at the top of the lost list. She comes twice a year, every
six months, four visits like clockwork — she is not lost, she is not due
until late October. Meanwhile Marisol Quintana has been in every four weeks
for two years and has now been silent for ten — two and a half times her own
gap, the clearest goodbye in the whole file — and she is not on the list at
all, because ten weeks is less than ninety days. One rule for both of them
cannot work. Ninety days is three missed visits for one woman and no missed
visits for the other.

FIX — judge every customer against their own cadence:
- A customer's gaps are the day-counts between their consecutive visits.
  Their usual gap is the MEDIAN of those gaps — with an even number of gaps,
  the average of the middle two.
- Cadence needs evidence: a customer needs at least min_visits visits
  (default 3) before we claim to know their rhythm.
- LAPSED: quiet longer than lapse_multiplier x their usual gap
  (default 2.5, and never less than 30 days).
- SLIPPING: not lapsed, but quiet longer than slipping_multiplier x their
  usual gap (default 1.5, never less than 14 days). These are the ones a
  message reaches in time.
- ONE visit only: they are not "lapsed", they never started. Put them in
  their own NEVER-CAME-BACK list once they are quiet past
  one_time_quiet_days (default 90); newer than that, they are TOO NEW to
  call. A first-timer gets a different message and an honest owner knows it.
- Two visits, or fewer than min_visits: TOO NEW / LOW DATA. Say so instead
  of guessing.
- Make all four knobs settings on the page, with the defaults above.
- "Today" is the newest date in the export — print it on the report as
  "as of", because a file exported in June must not make everyone look lapsed
  in September.
- The recovery projection: lapsed yearly value x a win-back rate the user
  sets (default 10%), labelled as the estimate it is. Do not promise.

Keep the ranking (yearly value, biggest first) inside each list, show their
usual gap in plain words on every row, and keep the message generator —
but it should now mention how long it has been in the customer's own terms.
